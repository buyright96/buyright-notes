How we check a deal

A sale label is easy to print. These are the four things we look at before a deal goes on this page, in the order that saves the most time.

  • Did we see it twice? We observed this offer on two checks at least 24 hours apart. It can still change or end. Two sightings rule out a one-hour flicker, not a short run: a price that ends early was short, not fake. Anything that moved between checks is dropped.
  • Who is selling it? We match the exact item, variant, new condition, seller and fulfillment shown on the offer. A brand's storefront page does not prove the brand is the seller on the offer you tap; the "Sold by" and "Ships from" lines do. Third-party sellers are a normal part of Amazon; a good one shows a long history and clear returns terms.
  • What does it usually sell for? The crossed-out number is the list price, which nobody may have paid this year. Compare against last month's price; a price-history tool shows that in one chart.
  • Would we buy it at full price? If not, a discount does not change the answer.

Before you buy anything on sale

The checks above cover the offer. These cover whether the product is right for you, which matters more.

  • Match the model number, seller and warranty. Search the maker's site for the model number in the listing title; sale listings often mix an older or smaller version in with the current one. Confirm the condition says New, open the seller's name to see who handles returns, and read the maker's warranty page: some makers cover any new unit, others only units from authorized sellers.
  • Measure first. Counter depth, cabinet height, the spot under the desk. A sale price does not make a 17-inch machine fit under a 16-inch cabinet.
  • Read what is in the box. Brush heads, filters, pods, a cover: the deal bundle sometimes has fewer than the standard one, and sometimes more.
  • Check the delivery date, not the Prime logo. Prime shipping is fast, but event-week stock runs out; the date shown at checkout is the one that counts.
  • Start small if you are not sure. A cheap item you already know you need makes the first order easy; add the bigger one once you trust the seller.
  • Know the return window. Amazon posts the current return policy on every listing; read it before you buy anything you cannot test in a store.

Questions people ask

When are Prime Big Deal Days 2026?

October 6 and 7, 2026. Early deals start appearing in the week before, marked as early Prime deals on the listing.

What does the "Checked" date under a deal mean?

The day we last observed that offer, not today's availability. It can change or end after that date; open the listing to see what it says now.

Do I need Prime to get the prices?

Yes for the Prime-exclusive deals, which is most of them. Some listings show a lower price to everyone; the listing says when a deal is Prime exclusive.

Is a Prime Big Deal Days price the lowest of the year?

Not always. Some items go lower on Black Friday, and Amazon devices are often close to the same price at both. If you can wait, compare against last November in a price-history tool.

Should I wait for Black Friday instead?

If you need the item before Thanksgiving, buy it now if the deal passes the checks above. If it is a gift you can hold, waiting costs little and may save more.

Which compact espresso setup should I buy?

Home espressoSeptember 26, 2026

A compact espresso setup works when three things fit together: the machine fits the counter, the grinder can reach an espresso grind, and the accessories match the machine's basket size. Pick in that order.

1. The machine: two picks, one catch each

Breville Bambino (our note). About 6.3 in wide with a 47 oz tank that lifts out at the back, heats in about 3 seconds, and takes 54 mm baskets and tampers, which are easy to find. The pick for a small kitchen that wants cafe-style milk drinks. Skip it if you want a built-in grinder.

CASABREWS CM5418 (our note). The starter machine: 20-bar pump, steam wand, 34 oz tank, a smaller price. The catch: it takes 51 mm baskets, and most accessory kits are made for 54 or 58 mm. Buy accessories by model name, not by "universal".

Both are compact enough for an 18-inch stretch of counter once the grinder is elsewhere. Measure first: Will an espresso machine fit my kitchen?

2. The grinder decides more than the machine

Espresso needs a much finer, narrower grind range than drip coffee. A grinder that cannot reach and adjust inside that range is the bottleneck, whatever the machine can do.

  • You can wait on a grinder if your machine ships with a pressurized (dual-wall) basket and you are happy with pre-ground espresso while you learn.
  • Buy the grinder first if you want fresh beans, a non-pressurized basket, or the ability to fix a shot that runs too fast or too slow. That fix is a grind change.

Our pick: Baratza Encore ESP (our note). It has a dedicated espresso range plus a coarser range for other brew methods, ships with a 54 mm dosing cup and a 58 mm adapter, and is quiet enough for an early house. Confirm your basket size before ordering.

3. Whole bean or pre-ground?

  • Whole bean if you own an espresso-capable grinder. Grind close to brewing; store the bag sealed, away from heat, light and moisture.
  • Pre-ground if you want the fewest steps or your machine has a pressurized basket. Buy coffee sold for espresso or a fine grind, and accept that you cannot make small adjustments when a shot tastes sour or bitter.
  • Never push a drip grind through a non-pressurized espresso basket and expect a predictable result.

"Espresso roast" describes a flavor direction, not a compatibility guarantee. Many coffees work for espresso; start with tasting notes you already like.

The order that avoids returns

  1. Measure the counter and the tank path.
  2. Choose the machine and note its basket size (51, 54 or 58 mm): look up your machine.
  3. Decide the grind plan: grinder now, or pre-ground with a pressurized basket.
  4. Only then buy accessories, by exact model.

# First-Week Espresso Setup Checklist: What to Verify Before Your Machine Arrives

Most expensive espresso mistakes happen before the first shot: a machine is ordered without space for the water tank, accessories are bought for the wrong portafilter size, or a bag of coffee arrives without a workable grind plan. This checklist keeps the first week simple.

## 1. Measure the counter, not just the machine

Write down width, depth, and vertical clearance. Then add the room needed to fill or remove the water tank, open cabinets, swing the steam wand, and safely reach the outlet. Use the [Countertop Espresso Machine Size Planner](../countertop-espresso-machine-size-planner/) for the measurement sequence.

## 2. Decide your grind plan before coffee arrives

If you want to use whole beans, confirm that you have an espresso-capable grinder. If you want fewer moving parts at first, confirm what grind and basket type your machine can accommodate. The [Whole Bean or Pre-Ground Coffee for Espresso](../whole-bean-or-pre-ground-coffee-for/) guide breaks down the trade-off.

## 3. Confirm portafilter size before buying tools

Tampers, dosing funnels, baskets, puck screens, and other accessories are not universal. Confirm the exact size and machine model before purchasing any of them. Start with the [51 mm vs. 54 mm vs. 58 mm compatibility guide](../51-vs-54-vs-58-mm-espresso-accessories/).

## 4. Buy one small bag of coffee, not a cabinet full

Choose a flavor direction you enjoy, get a manageable bag size, and use it to learn your setup. The goal is not instant perfection. It is a repeatable starting point you can adjust. See [Buying Your First Espresso Beans](../buying-your-first-espresso-beans-roast/) for a simple first-bag plan.

## 5. Keep the first tool list short

For a basic espresso workflow, the machine manual controls. Add tools only when they solve a specific problem: a scale for repeatable recipes, a compatible tamper if your machine did not include one, a milk pitcher if you make milk drinks, and cleaning supplies your manufacturer specifically calls for.

## 6. Use your machine manual as the final authority

This checklist is for planning. Follow the machine’s exact instructions for electrical setup, cleaning, water use, basket compatibility, and descaling.

## Finish line

You are ready to begin when you can answer four questions: Does the machine fit with access clearance? Do I have a workable grind plan? Do my accessories fit this machine? Do I have a small bag of coffee I can learn with?

# Fall baking setup

Three tools cover most fall baking: something to mix, something to bake bread in, and something to measure with. Here is when each one is worth it, and when it is not.

## Stand mixer or hand mixer?

A stand mixer pays off when you make big batches or stiff doughs: a 5-quart bowl mixes about 9 dozen cookies in one go, and the machine kneads bread dough while you do something else.

- **Get the stand mixer** if you bake most weeks, make bread or cookie dough in quantity, or want the attachments later (pasta, grinder).
- **A hand mixer is enough** if you bake a few times a year, mostly cakes, whipped cream and quick breads. It costs a fraction and lives in a drawer.
- What to check on any stand mixer: bowl size against your usual batch, tilt-head or bowl-lift (tilt-head is easier to add ingredients to and fits under most cabinets), and counter space, because a stand mixer stays out.

The KitchenAid Artisan is the tilt-head 5-quart model with 10 speeds, from a slow stir to whipping cream, in more than 20 colors. Skip it if you bake only a few times a year.

## Bread in a Dutch oven

Crusty no-knead bread needs a heavy, lidded pot that holds heat and traps steam. That is exactly what an enameled cast-iron Dutch oven does, and it doubles for soups, stews and braises all season.

- **Oven-safe temperature** is the spec that matters for bread: the Lodge 6-quart is oven-safe to 500°F, hot enough for a crusty loaf.
- **Enamel** means no seasoning and no rust worries; hand washing is recommended, and it is dishwasher safe.
- **6 quarts** fits a standard loaf and a family-size stew. Bigger is heavier, and cast iron is already heavy when full: if lifting a big pot is hard for you, that is the reason to skip it.

Preheat the pot with the oven, drop the dough in on parchment, lid on for the first half, lid off to brown.

## Weigh, do not scoop

Most baking failures come from measuring flour by the cup: a packed cup can weigh a third more than a fluffed one. A scale that reads in 1 g steps fixes that.

- The Escali Primo reads to 1 g up to 11 lb, with two buttons, tare and unit (g, oz, lb:oz), a sealed panel that shrugs off spills, and auto-off after 4 minutes.
- Put the bowl on, press tare, add each ingredient to weight. No measuring cups to wash.
- Skip it if you need tenths of a gram (coffee dosing, small-batch candy); that needs a 0.1 g scale.

## What can wait

- A second bowl, a pasta roller, a dough hook upgrade: wait until the recipe you actually make asks for them.
- A bread lame, a banneton, a pizza stone: nice, not needed for a first no-knead loaf.
- A second Dutch oven: one 6-quart pot does bread and dinner; buy a second size only when they compete for the oven.

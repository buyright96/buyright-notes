# Will an espresso machine fit my kitchen?

Most espresso regrets happen before the first shot: the machine fits on paper, then you cannot refill the tank without dragging it out. Ten minutes with a tape measure settles it.

## Measure these five things

1. **Usable counter width.** Not wall to wall: the clear stretch where the machine will live, with room beside it for a cup, a tamper and, if you grind fresh, the grinder.
2. **Depth.** From the backsplash to the safe front edge. Leave a gap behind the machine for the cord and for rear-fill tanks.
3. **Height under the cabinets.** Top-fill tanks and lids need open air above the machine to open and lift out.
4. **The refill path.** Find where the tank sits: top, rear, or a side tank that slides out. Can you reach it, lift it and put it back without moving the machine?
5. **The cord and the outlet.** A safe path to power, away from the sink and the drip tray.

Write the numbers down. Machine listings give the body size; only your numbers tell you whether the station will work.

## What the tank position means

- **Top-fill:** works under cabinets only if you have clearance to open the lid and lift the tank.
- **Rear-fill:** needs a real gap behind the machine. Pushed against the backsplash, every refill becomes a chore.
- **Removable side or front tank:** the most forgiving, as long as shelves and cabinets do not block its path.

Do not plan on sliding the machine out daily. Water, hot metal, a cord and a crowded counter make that a bad routine.

## Buy now, wait, or skip

Before checkout, write two lines: the problem this machine must solve, and the one constraint that could make it unusable.

- **Buy now** when the need is immediate, the measurements fit with room to spare, and the return policy is clear.
- **Wait** when one measurement or compatibility detail is still missing. Get it first; it is cheaper than a return.
- **Skip** when the machine adds work without solving the original problem.

## Then check the model, not the brand

Confirm the exact model number and read its current dimensions and manual before ordering. Same-brand machines differ by generation, and the manual is the final word on clearances and water.

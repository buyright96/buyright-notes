# A reading nook that helps you sleep

The phone is the problem: notifications, blue light, one more scroll. A reading nook is three small changes that make the bed a place you read and then sleep.

## Read on something that is not a phone

An e-reader is a book with a light in it. The things that matter at bedtime:

- **A glare-free screen with a warm light.** The Kindle Paperwhite's 7-inch screen shifts from white to amber, so late reading does not feel like staring at a monitor.
- **No apps.** No social media, no notifications, nothing to check. That is the feature.
- **Battery in weeks, not hours.** Up to 12 weeks on one USB-C charge, per Amazon, so it lives on the nightstand and rarely needs the cable.

Skip it if you love the feel of paper books; a bedside lamp and a stack of paperbacks do the same job.

## The cover that fits

Kindle covers are model-specific, and the naming trips people up. The fabric cover on our list fits the **2024 Kindle Paperwhite and Paperwhite Signature Edition, and the Kindle Colorsoft and Colorsoft Signature Edition**. It does not fit the 2021-and-earlier Paperwhite or the basic Kindle.

- Find your model in the Kindle's Settings under Device Info, or on the order page, before buying any cover.
- A good cover wakes and sleeps the reader when opened and closed, folds flat behind for one-handed reading, and stays on for charging.
- It adds about 5 oz; the point of an e-reader is that it is light, so skip bulky folio cases with keyboards or stands.

## Light and sound for winding down

The last piece is the alarm. A buzzer at 6 a.m. undoes a calm night; a sunrise alarm brightens gradually before it sounds, and the same device plays sleep sounds at night.

- The Hatch Restore 3 has a dimmable clock face, 80+ sleep sounds per Hatch, and a sunrise alarm. Its wind-down routines run through the Hatch app; core features work without the subscription, but new users get a 30-day Hatch+ trial and prompts to keep it.
- Skip it if you would rather not use an app: setup and most routines happen there.
- Put it where the light reaches your face at wake-up time, not behind the e-reader stand.

## Setting it up

1. Charge the e-reader once, set the warm light to come on at sunset, and turn off the lock-screen ads if your model has them.
2. Put the cover on and check the wake/sleep works when you open and close it.
3. Set the sunrise alarm for 20 to 30 minutes before you need to be up, and pick one sleep sound; do not change it nightly.
4. Move the phone charger out of the bedroom. That is most of the benefit.
